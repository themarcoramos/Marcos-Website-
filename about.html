Marco A. Ramos Jr.

Policy & Government Relations Professional | MPA

I grew up in Ivanhoe, California, a small farm town in the Central Valley, and I was a first-generation student. I learned early to navigate rooms where underestimation was the baseline. That gave me a clear compass: whether it’s a piece of state legislation, a court docket, or an internal database, public systems should protect human dignity.

Today I work in Sacramento, where law, data, and people meet. I analyze California bills, testify before legislative committees, and build the software and data tools that turn policy into something people can use. Before CAMHPRO, I worked in a California superior court, where I audited case-flow operations across a 5,000+ litigant caseload.

CAMHPRO. I track and analyze California legislation and oppose expansions of involuntary commitment through committee testimony and coalition advocacy. I also run digital outreach, including an automated letter tool that has sent 1,500+ messages on 17 bills so community voices reach lawmakers.

Big Brothers Big Sisters of the Greater Sacramento Area. I designed and built the enrollment and compliance-tracking platform the organization now uses, serve as its lead IT administrator, and helped design and launch Sports Bigs, a grant-funded mentoring initiative for Sacramento County students.

I have a Master of Public Administration from California State University, Long Beach (4.0 GPA) and a B.A. in political science from California State University, Fresno (magna cum laude). For an MPA project, I built a Python (Pandas/NumPy) fiscal audit engine and applied it to the public Form 990 filings of the American Bar Association Fund for Justice and Education. I’m bilingual in English and Spanish, which keeps my work close to the communities I serve.

Outside Work

I play trombone in a community symphony. Playing low brass taught me deep listening, dynamic balance, and knowing when to step forward and when to support the ensemble. I also skate distance on roller and ice skates, lift in the gym, and play Rainbow Six Siege in the top 1% of players. I try to approach my work the way I approach a piece of music or a high-stakes match: with steady focus and respect for the people in the room.
